\section{A fixed synthetic three-site benchmark}
\label{sec:realdata}

The supplied node files are synthetic. We retain
this fixed benchmark with 800 training and 200 validation observations,
600 predictors, and site sizes $(400,240,160)$ and $(100,60,40)$,
respectively. Its original generation procedure, seed and coefficient
vector were not supplied, so the generating support cannot be evaluated.
The matrices are used without further preprocessing. Their unknown
generation details preclude claims about the site distributions.
The original files and their SHA-256 checksums are retained with the code,
so this analysis can be repeated from the fixed inputs even though new
datasets from the original generator cannot be reproduced.
This is a descriptive computational example, not an observational application.
Agreement refers to the pooled minimum-MSE Lasso support, not correctness.

Local penalties are $(\Slamone,\Slamtwo,\Slamthree)$, giving
$\bar\lambda=\Slambar$. The pooled reference selects penalty
$\Slampooled$ and retains \Srefactive\ predictors.

At $E=1$, FedAvg retains \Sfedactive\ predictors with agreement
$F_1=\SfedFone$, compared with \SlocalFone\ for the largest-site fit.
ST retains \Sstactive\ predictors with $F_1=\SstFone$; P retains
\Spactive\ with $F_1=\SpFone$. ADMM at $\bar\lambda$ retains
\Sadmmactive\ and gives $F_1=\SadmmFone$.
ADMM-CV under minimum-MSE selection has the same support as the pooled
reference in this run, but slightly different coefficients and penalty.
The pooled-objective identity guarantees equal solutions only at equal
penalties. Neither support agreement nor its increase validates the
unknown generating support. Pooled one-SE tuning retains \SpoolseAct\
predictors, illustrating the dependence of the reference itself on tuning.

Thresholding reduces variation in active-set size across $E$, while ST's
agreement declines from \SstFone\ at $E=1$ to \SstFtwenty\ at
$E=20$. Its $F_1$ range is larger than that of plain averaging. Thus a
flatter size curve cannot be interpreted as stability of the selected
variables, and this one benchmark cannot establish the cause.

ADMM attains $F=\Sadmmobj$, versus \Sfedobj\ for FedAvg at $E=1$:
a gap of \Sgappct\ for the specified common objective. The FedAvg
objective is smallest at $E=2$ (\Sfedobjtwo) and reaches
\Sfedobjtwenty\ at $E=20$. Meanwhile rounds fall from \Sroundsone\
to \Sroundstwenty, upload from \Skibone\ to \Skibtwenty~KiB,
and local CD work increases from \Sepochsone\ to \Sepochstwenty\
sweeps per site. These are separate optimisation and cost comparisons.
P's released cost totals include all five threshold candidates; its
selected-fit costs are also supplied. No distributed tuning cost is
assigned to the centrally evaluated ADMM-CV paths.
