\section{Illustration on a three-site data set}
\label{sec:realdata}

We close with an illustration on the data set distributed with this
article: a high-dimensional continuous-response regression sample with
$n = 800$ training and $200$ validation observations on $p = 600$
predictors, partitioned into $K = 3$ disjoint sites of sizes
$(400, 240, 160)$ with validation splits $(100, 60, 40)$. The
data-generating process and the true coefficient vector are not available
to us, so, unlike in Section~\ref{sec:sim}, no ground-truth support exists:
the pooled Lasso fit is used as the \emph{reference} support and all
agreement metrics are agreement with that estimator, not correctness. The
partition is computational rather than institutional, so the sites differ
only in size; this design isolates the aggregation effect from genuine
between-site heterogeneity.

Locally selected penalties were $\lambda = (\Slamone, \Slamtwo, \Slamthree)$
for sites $1$--$3$, giving $\bar\lambda = \Slambar$; the pooled reference
selected $\Slampooled$ and retained $\Srefactive$ active predictors.

Table~\ref{tab:real} reproduces the pattern of Section~\ref{sec:results} on
these data. Plain averaging at $E = 1$ retains $\Sfedactive$ of $600$
predictors---more than twice the reference---and its agreement with the
reference is $F_1 = \SfedFone$, barely above the $\SlocalFone$ obtained by
fitting the Lasso at the largest site alone and ignoring the other two.
Adding the server-side threshold cuts the active set to $\Sstactive$ and
raises $F_1$ to $\SstFone$; per-round proximal aggregation gives
$\SpFone$ with $\Spactive$ active. Consensus ADMM at $\bar\lambda$ retains
$\Sadmmactive$ predictors, of which $\Sadmmtp$ are in the reference support
and $\Sadmmfp$ are not, for $F_1 = \SadmmFone$; selecting the consensus
penalty by validation instead of inheriting $\bar\lambda$ gives
$F_1 = \SadmmcvFone$ with $\SadmmcvAct$ active, which again shows how much
of the apparent gain is the penalty rather than the solver. Under the
minimum-MSE rule \textsc{ADMM-CV} reproduces the pooled reference exactly,
which is the expected consequence of Lemma~\ref{lem:pooled} and serves here
as a numerical check rather than as a result; its agreement metrics are
degenerate by construction and should be read as such. Under matched
one-standard-error tuning the centralised reference itself keeps only
$\SpoolseAct$ predictors.

One pattern here differs from the simulations and we flag it rather than
smooth it over. In Section~\ref{sec:results} the thresholded schedules are
nearly flat in $E$; on these data \textsc{FedAvg-ST} falls from
$F_1 = \SstFone$ at $E = 1$ to $\SstFtwenty$ at $E = 20$. Thresholding
therefore does not universally remove the $E$-dependence of the recovered
support---it removes it in the designs we simulated, and here it only
attenuates it. With a single data set and no replication we cannot say which
feature of these data is responsible.

The objective values make the optimisation gap concrete. Consensus ADMM
attains $F = \Sadmmobj$; plain averaging at $E = 1$ stops at
$F = \Sfedobj$, or $\Sgappct$ above the minimum of the very objective it is
constructed to target. The gap is not monotone in $E$: it is smallest at
$E = 2$ ($F = \Sfedobjtwo$) and then widens steadily to $\Sfedobjtwenty$ at
$E = 20$. Its variation across the whole grid, $\Sfedobjrange$, is about a
fifth of its distance from the minimum, $\Sgapabs$, which is the point:
choosing $E$ moves the objective far less than choosing how to aggregate. The familiar $E$ effects are present as
well---rounds fall from $\Sroundsone$ to $\Sroundstwenty$ and upload from
$\Skibone$ to $\Skibtwenty$~KiB, while total local work rises from
$\Sepochsone$ to $\Sepochstwenty$ coordinate-descent epochs per site---but
they operate on an estimator that is, at every $E$, far from the solution of
its own optimisation problem.
