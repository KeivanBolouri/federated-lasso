\subsection{Averaging and local penalty selection}

With minimum-MSE local penalties, FedAvg selects \MIndFedAct,
\MCorFedAct, and \MHetFedAct\ variables at $E=1$ in the independent,
correlated, and heterogeneous designs. Its recalls are \MIndFedRec,
\MCorFedRec, and \MHetFedRec, but precisions are only \MIndFedPrec,
\MCorFedPrec, and \MHetFedPrec. The average retains substantially more
variables than the true model; this is compatible with support enlargement
through aggregation, rather than evidence that every coefficient is nonzero.

Increasing $E$ from 1 to 20 reduces active-set size by \MIndActDrop,
\MCorActDrop, and \MHetActDrop, and increases $F_1$ from \MIndFedF\ to
\MIndFedTF, \MCorFedF\ to \MCorFedTF, and \MHetFedF\ to \MHetFedTF.
The large-$E$ results approach one-shot averaging, whose $F_1$ values are
\MIndOneF, \MCorOneF, and \MHetOneF. These empirical changes do not
identify a unique mechanism: the Gram matrices are nonorthogonal, the
site fits differ, and the aggregation map changes with $E$.

Changing the local penalty rule improves averaging without adding a server
threshold. FedAvg-1SE gives $F_1=\MIndFedSeF$, \MCorFedSeF, and
\MHetFedSeF\ at $E=1$, and \MIndFedSeTF, \MCorFedSeTF, and
\MHetFedSeTF\ at $E=20$. One-shot-1SE gives \MIndOneSeF,
\MCorOneSeF, and \MHetOneSeF. These controls show why a comparison
between prediction-tuned averaging and sparsity-favouring thresholding
cannot attribute the entire difference to aggregation alone.

\subsection{Thresholding, prediction and epoch effects}

FedAvg-ST attains $F_1=\MIndStF$ (MCSE \MIndStSe), \MCorStF\
(\MCorStSe), and \MHetStF\ (\MHetStSe) at $E=1$, with active-set
sizes \MIndStAct, \MCorStAct, and \MHetStAct. FedAvg-P attains
\MIndProxF, \MCorProxF, and \MHetProxF\ at $E=1$, rising to
\MIndProxTF, \MCorProxTF, and \MHetProxTF\ at $E=20$.
The two thresholding procedures therefore have different epoch responses.

Table~\ref{tab:paired} quantifies these effects using within-replicate
contrasts. ST has a modest decline in the homogeneous designs that is
resolved relative to its paired Monte Carlo uncertainty, while the
heterogeneous-design contrast is much smaller. P has positive contrasts
in all three designs. These results do not support general independence
of thresholded selection from $E$. A small change in mean $F_1$ also does
not establish identical selected variables within a replicate.

Selection gains can come with increased estimation and prediction error.
For example, in the independent design at $E=1$, moving from FedAvg to ST
changes coefficient error from \MIndFedErr\ to \MIndStErr\ and test MSE
from \MIndFedMse\ to \MIndStMse. The preferred estimator therefore
depends on whether variable selection, estimation, or prediction is the
primary objective. These metrics should be reported together.

\subsection{Optimisation accuracy and statistical performance}

Pooled minimum-MSE tuning gives $F_1=\MIndPoolF$, \MCorPoolF, and
\MHetPoolF, compared with \MIndPoolSeF, \MCorPoolSeF, and
\MHetPoolSeF\ under one-SE tuning. The corresponding ADMM-CV values
are \MIndAdmmCvF, \MCorAdmmCvF, and \MHetAdmmCvF, versus
\MIndAdmmCvSeF, \MCorAdmmCvSeF, and \MHetAdmmCvSeF\ with one SE.
The tuning rule can change support recovery more than the epoch contrasts
considered here.

ADMM at the inherited penalty gives $F_1=\MIndAdmmF$, \MCorAdmmF,
and \MHetAdmmF. Its favourable selection results in the homogeneous
designs depend partly on the inherited penalty. In the heterogeneous
design, retuning the common penalty by one SE improves $F_1$ from
\MHetAdmmF\ to \MHetAdmmCvSeF. This does not establish that a common
penalty is intrinsically inadequate for heterogeneous sites. In particular,
ST's threshold is also common to all coordinates and sites, and the present
experiments do not isolate a site-adaptation mechanism.

A converged pooled Lasso minimises its specified penalised training
objective. It does not necessarily maximise $F_1$ or minimise test error.
Consequently a thresholded estimator can outperform it on a statistical
metric while having a larger value of $F$.

\subsection{Communication and local work}

\begin{figure}[t]

\caption{FedAvg upload (solid, left axis) and CD sweeps per site (dashed,

excluding initial local penalty search and downloads.}
\label{fig:cost}

For minimum-MSE FedAvg in the independent design, increasing $E$ from 1
to 20 reduces rounds from \MIndRoundsOne\ to \MIndRoundsTwenty,
but increases CD work per site from \MIndEpochsOne\ to
\MIndEpochsTwenty. The correlated and heterogeneous designs also
show this trade-off (\MCorEpochsOne\ to \MCorEpochsTwenty, and
\MHetEpochsOne\ to \MHetEpochsTwenty\ CD sweeps, respectively).
A communication-efficient schedule need not be computation-efficient.

Post-fit ST adds only \MIndStScalar~KiB of validation scalars to its
underlying fit. P instead requires all five candidate trajectories. In
the independent design at $E=1$, its selected fit averages
\MIndProxSelectedRoundsOne\ rounds, whereas all candidates total
\MIndProxAllRoundsOne\ rounds and \MIndProxUploadOne~KiB including
validation summaries. At $E=20$ these totals are
\MIndProxAllRoundsTwenty\ rounds and \MIndProxUploadTwenty~KiB.
Reporting only the selected P fit would materially understate the cost
of obtaining that estimator. ADMM uses \MIndAdmmRounds,
\MCorAdmmRounds, and \MHetAdmmRounds\ rounds at its stricter residual
tolerance; differing stopping criteria must be considered when interpreting
these costs.
