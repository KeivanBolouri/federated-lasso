\section{Public diabetes-data illustration}
\label{sec:publicdata}

The publicly distributed diabetes regression data contain $442$ patients
and ten baseline predictors: age, sex, body mass index, blood pressure,
and six serum measurements. The response measures disease progression
one year after baseline \citep{efron2004}. We use the unscaled data bundled
with scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011}, accessed with
\texttt{load\_diabetes(scaled=False)}. The archived CSV, source links,
package version and checksum accompany the code.
This is a low-dimensional observational-data illustration, separate from
the high-dimensional generated-data experiments.

For each of $100$ random splits, we assign $260$ observations to training,
$80$ to validation and $102$ to testing. Training site sizes are
$(130,78,52)$ and validation site sizes are $(40,24,16)$; these are
simulated partitions, not separate clinical institutions.
Split $r=0,\ldots,99$ uses the permutation from NumPy's
\texttt{default\_rng(20260911+r)}; all row assignments are archived.
Predictor means and standard deviations and the response mean are fitted
using training observations only. They define a common feature scale and
intercept for all methods, including the largest-site fits; no test
observations enter preprocessing, fitting or tuning. The common
transformation can be obtained from site-level counts and sums.

Local penalties and the threshold grids follow
Section~\ref{sec:method}, with $E\in\{1,3,20\}$.
Pooled and largest-site fits use both minimum-MSE and one-standard-error
tuning. ADMM uses $\bar\lambda$.
Support agreement is measured against the pooled one-standard-error fit
within the same split, not an assumed true support. A separate descriptive
stability measure averages pairwise Jaccard overlap between a method's
selected sets over the $\binom{100}{2}$ pairs of splits. Larger overlap
does not establish that a selected variable is relevant.
The test MSE is on the original response scale. Its reported standard
deviation describes variation across splits of the same observations;
it is not an independent-sample standard error. Averaging methods stop at
an update norm below $10^{-5}$ or at $500$ rounds. Any returned capped
iterates are retained and flagged; validation also retains every candidate
threshold. ADMM is allowed up to $20{,}000$ iterations and must meet its
residual criterion.
