\begin{abstract}
Federated Lasso procedures must choose how many local updates occur between communication rounds. We study this choice for cyclic coordinate descent, separating aggregation, penalty selection, and computational cost. Under orthogonal local designs, one sweep determines the local fit, so the aggregated estimator is independent of the epoch count. Its support generally equals the union of local supports, and its fixed point need not minimise the pooled Lasso objective. Simulations with 200 replicates per design compare averaging, server thresholding, and consensus optimisation under explicit tuning rules. Both local penalty selection and server thresholding affect support recovery; improvements can increase prediction error. Communication accounting includes all candidate trajectories used to select per-round thresholds. Additional experiments compare a deterministic federated dual-averaging implementation at fixed training budgets and examine public diabetes data. The results distinguish active-set size from variable identity and show why communication, computation, optimisation error, and selection quality require careful separate assessment.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} federated learning; Lasso; coordinate descent; variable selection; communication cost; dual averaging.
