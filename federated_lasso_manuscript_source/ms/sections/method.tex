\section{A server-side selection step, and exact comparators}
\label{sec:method}

Proposition~\ref{prop:orth}(iv) identifies a specific defect---the
aggregation step returns a dense vector---and suggests an equally specific
remedy: apply the proximal operator of the $\ell_1$ penalty at the server,
where the aggregation happens.

\subsection{\textsc{FedAvg-ST}}

Let $\bbeta_R$ be the output of Algorithm~\ref{alg:fedavg} after $R$ rounds.
\textsc{FedAvg-ST} adds a single round in which the server broadcasts a grid
of candidate thresholds $\tau \in \mathcal{T}$, each site returns the two
scalars $\sum_i (y_{ji} - x_{ji}^\top \soft(\bbeta_R,\tau))^2$ and its own
$m_j^{\mathrm{val}}$ for each candidate, and the server selects
\begin{equation}
\hat\tau = \max\Big\{\tau \in \mathcal{T} :
  \widehat{\mathrm{MSE}}(\tau) \le
  \widehat{\mathrm{MSE}}(\tau_{\min}) + \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}(\tau_{\min})\Big\},
\qquad
\hat\bbeta^{\mathrm{ST}} = \soft(\bbeta_R, \hat\tau),
\label{eq:st}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\mathrm{MSE}}(\tau)$ is the weighted validation mean squared
error, $\tau_{\min}$ its minimiser over the grid, and
$\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}$ its standard error. This is the familiar
one-standard-error rule applied to a threshold rather than to a penalty; the
minimum-MSE rule is deliberately \emph{not} used, because validation MSE is
nearly flat in $\tau$ near zero and selects $\hat\tau \approx 0$, returning
the dense iterate unchanged. The step costs one additional communication
round, transmits $2|\mathcal{T}|$ scalars per site instead of a
$p$-vector, and requires no row-level exchange.

Two things should be said plainly about what this does and does not fix.
It restores sparsity, and Section~\ref{sec:results} shows it recovers a
large share of the selection accuracy lost to averaging. It does not
address the optimisation bias of Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed}: thresholding
a point that is not a minimiser of $F$ does not make it one. It is a
post-processing device with a clear justification, not a corrected
algorithm.

\subsection{Comparators}

We compare against four alternatives, all fitted on identical data.

\paragraph{Pooled Lasso.} The centralised fit on the concatenated sample,
with its penalty tuned by validation MSE on the concatenated validation
split. This is the infeasible reference an analyst would use if the data
could be centralised. Note that it is tuned independently and therefore
generally uses a penalty different from $\bar\lambda$.

\paragraph{Consensus ADMM.} The alternating direction method of multipliers
in global consensus form \citep{boyd2011} applied to $F$: each site solves a
ridge-regularised least-squares subproblem by a Cholesky factorisation
computed once, the server soft-thresholds the averaged primal-plus-dual
variable, and duals are updated locally. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pooled} this
returns the pooled Lasso solution at $\bar\lambda$ to numerical precision,
using the same communication pattern (one $p$-vector per site per
iteration) and the same privacy boundary as
Algorithm~\ref{alg:fedavg}. It is therefore the right control: it isolates
the effect of \emph{how} the sites are combined, holding the objective, the
data and the communication channel fixed.

\paragraph{One-shot averaging.} Each site solves its local problem to
convergence and the server averages once, the $E \to \infty$, $R = 1$
extreme of Algorithm~\ref{alg:fedavg}
\citep{lee2017,battey2018}.

\paragraph{Local-only.} The Lasso fitted at the largest site alone, with no
communication. This is the do-nothing baseline, and it is the relevant one:
a federated procedure that does not beat it is not worth deploying.

\subsection{Reporting conventions}

A coefficient is called active when $|\hat\bbeta_k| > \epsilon$ with
$\epsilon = 10^{-4}$; because the aggregated iterate carries many
small non-zero entries, results for plain averaging are sensitive to
$\epsilon$ and we report that sensitivity explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. Support quality is summarised by precision,
recall, $F_1$ and the Jaccard index against the true support in simulation,
and against the pooled Lasso support in the data illustration, where the
truth is unknown. Estimation quality is $\|\hat\bbeta - \bbeta^\star\|_2$
and prediction quality is mean squared error on an independent test sample
of $2000$ observations. Cost is reported on both axes: upload in KiB and
total coordinate-descent epochs per site.
